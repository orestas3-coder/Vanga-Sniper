The Vanga rule: We don’t trade “signals”. We trade the sequence: Liquidity → Shift → Value → Trigger.

Philosophy

Liquidity is taken → structure shifts → price returns to value → we enter with tight invalidation.

What we optimize

  • Clean entries (tight SL) from value.
  • High probability only (clear shift + zone).
  • Same rules every day. No freestyle.

What we avoid

  • Entering without sweep/shift.
  • Entering mid-move (no value).
  • Chop / mixed structure.

Structure (BOS / CHoCH)

BOS

Break of Structure = close beyond a meaningful swing level. We trade the retest.

CHoCH

Change of Character = first break that changes behavior (often the start of a reversal).

Pro tip: Don’t trade the first break. Trade the retest into value after the break.

Value Zones (OB / FVG / OTE)

We only enter from value. If you enter mid-move, you’re usually late.

OB

Last strong candle before impulse. Best with shift + retest.

FVG

Imbalance from speed. Great for precise entries on mitigation.

OTE

Fib value zone (0.618–0.786) inside a leg. Best after sweep + shift.

HTF S/D

Highest priority zones for context and “space”.

Rule: If you can’t place a clean SL beyond structure without it being huge — you don’t have a value entry.

The 3 Core Setups

Setup 1 — Sell Day

PDH/BSL sweep → BOS down → retest OB/OTE short.

  • Sweep above PDH / equal highs.
  • Bearish shift (BOS/CHoCH).
  • Short only from value with rejection trigger.

Setup 2 — Buy Day

PDL/SSL sweep → BOS up → retest OB/OTE long.

  • Sweep below PDL / equal lows.
  • Bullish shift (BOS/CHoCH).
  • Buy only from value with confirmation candle.

Setup 3 — Trend continuation

HTF trend + counter-sweep → shift back with trend → entry from HTF value.

  • HTF bias must be clean.
  • Counter-sweep against the trend happens.
  • Enter on retest into value after shift back with the trend.

Trade Management

  • Plan entry/SL/TPs before you enter.
  • TP1 reduces risk, TP2 is the main liquidity pool.
  • Don’t move SL to BE instantly (BE is a magnet).
  • Time stop: if no follow-through, exit and reset.

Risk Rules

  • Minimum RRR: 1:2. If target is too close, skip.
  • SL beyond structure. If structure is unclear, risk is unclear.
  • Keep SL as small as structure allows. If it gets ugly, wait for a better retest.
  • Setups are a series. Execute the plan, don’t chase perfection.

No-Trade Rules

Skip if…

  • HTF is mixed.
  • No sweep.
  • No BOS/CHoCH.
  • Chop / low momentum.
  • News spike incoming.

One-line discipline

If you feel urgency, you’re probably late. Wait for the retest.

Glossary (click to expand)

These are the exact terms we use in the Vanga Sniper strategy. Each item includes: what it is, how we use it, and what to avoid.

Market Structure

BOS (Break of Structure)

What it is: A decisive break of a meaningful swing level (previous swing high in an up move, previous swing low in a down move).

How we use it: BOS is our “permission” that direction is real. We rarely enter on the breakout candle — we prefer the retest into value (OB / FVG / OTE) after the break.

What to avoid:

  • Weak break with no displacement or low volume (often a fake BOS).
  • Entering mid‑move after BOS (you get used as liquidity).
  • BOS into a higher‑timeframe supply/demand wall (bad location).

VPA filter: a true BOS usually shows “effort” (volume) matching “result” (distance/impulse). Big candle + low volume = suspicious.

CHoCH (Change of Character)

What it is: The first break that changes behavior (trend → pullback stops trending, or range → new direction starts). Often appears right after a liquidity sweep.

How we use it: CHoCH is the early warning. We want CHoCH + then a BOS in our direction, then entry from value.

What to avoid:

  • Calling every small poke a CHoCH — it must break a meaningful level.
  • Taking CHoCH against the higher‑timeframe bias without a sweep + strong displacement.
Internal vs Swing Structure

Internal structure = micro breaks inside the leg (often on M5/M15). Swing structure = the larger leg (often H1/H4).

  • Day trades: respect the swing direction first, use internal structure for timing.
  • Scalps: can use internal shifts, but only if you’re trading from a clean value zone with tight invalidation.

Rule of thumb: if internal says “buy” but swing is clearly bearish — that’s usually a pullback, not a new trend.

Displacement

What it is: A strong impulse candle sequence that moves price away from an area (often creates an FVG). It signals institutional participation.

How we use it: We want displacement after a sweep/shift. Then we wait for price to return to value (FVG/OB/OTE) for entry.

What to avoid: Slow grinding moves into a level (often means the level will break).

Liquidity

BSL / SSL (Buyside / Sellside Liquidity)

What it is: Clusters of stops. BSL sits above swing highs (shorts’ stops + buy stops). SSL sits below swing lows (longs’ stops + sell stops).

How we use it:

  • Expect sweeps near session opens (London / NY).
  • Targets: liquidity pools are natural magnets for TP2/TP3.

What to avoid:

  • Buying directly into BSL or selling directly into SSL (you’re late).
  • Ignoring “equal highs/lows” (most obvious liquidity).
Liquidity Sweep / Grab

What it is: Price runs a visible high/low (takes stops) and then rejects. This is the classic “trap” move.

How we use it: We prefer entries after the sweep, not before. The sweep sets the “why”, structure shift sets the “permission”, and the retest into value gives the “entry”.

VPA filter: sweep candle with strong wick + high volume can be real reversal intent. Wick + low volume can be a fake spike.

EQH / EQL (Equal Highs / Equal Lows)

What it is: Same‑price highs or lows where retail stops stack. This is premium liquidity.

How we use it: We mark them as likely sweep points or as TP magnets.

Inducement

What it is: A “fake” move that invites traders in the wrong direction before the real move.

How we use it: If price forms a clean inducement low/high before a major level, we expect it to be taken first, then the real move starts.

PDH / PDL / EQ (Previous Day High / Low / Mid)

What it is: Yesterday’s high/low and midpoint. They frame today’s liquidity.

  • PDH sweep + bearish shift → often “Sell Day” behavior.
  • PDL sweep + bullish shift → often “Buy Day” behavior.

Your dashboard prints PDH/PDL/EQ so you can anchor the day fast.

Premium / Discount

What it is: Relative value inside a range: premium = expensive (upper half), discount = cheap (lower half).

How we use it: We prefer shorts in premium and buys in discount, especially when aligned with BOS/CHoCH and zones.

Value Zones

OB (Order Block)

What it is: The “last candle(s)” before displacement. Vanga Sniper can show two OB styles:

  • Market‑Structure OB: printed after BOS/CHoCH (our favorite for entries).
  • Volume‑Pivot OB: OBs found via volume pivot logic (useful, but we still want structure + location).

How we use it: Entry on retest with tight SL beyond the OB boundary (wick/close mitigation depends on your setting).

What to avoid: Taking OBs in the middle of a range with no sweep/shift = coin flip.

Mitigation (Wick vs Close)

What it is: “Mitigation” means the market has returned to the zone and consumed it.

  • Wick mitigation: zone considered used when price wicks through.
  • Close mitigation: stricter; zone considered used only when price closes through.

How we use it: If a zone is mitigated, we reduce confidence in using it again.

FVG (Fair Value Gap / Imbalance)

What it is: A 3‑candle imbalance created by speed/displacement. Price often returns to “rebalance” it.

How we use it:

  • Entry = retest/fill of FVG after a shift.
  • Healthy retrace usually shows lower volume than the impulse.

What to avoid:

  • Buying/selling inside an FVG without structure confirmation.
  • Assuming every gap must fill immediately — context matters.
OTE (Optimal Trade Entry)

What it is: Fibonacci value zone (0.618–0.786) of the current swing leg.

How we use it: We use OTE as a value “box” — but we still require: (1) sweep/liquidity event, (2) shift/BOS, (3) trigger candle/mini shift inside the box.

What to avoid: Blindly shorting/buying OTE without context — OTE is not a signal by itself.

Supply / Demand Zones (with Strength Score)

What it is: Multi‑timeframe supply/demand boxes. Vanga Sniper scores zones by size vs ATR, age, and timeframe multiplier (0–100) and can highlight the strongest.

How we use it: Higher timeframe zones (H1/H4/D) matter more. We look for sweeps into them, then shifts, then entries from value.

What to avoid: Treating every zone the same — strongest HTF zones dominate.

Sessions, Timing & Dashboard Metrics

Killzones (London / Pre‑NY / NYSE / Asia)

What it is: Time windows where liquidity and volatility typically spike.

  • London: often the first real impulse / manipulation.
  • Pre‑NY & NYSE open: either continuation or reversal — many daily highs/lows form here.
  • Asia: often a range that later gets swept.

How we use it: We prefer to execute after a sweep inside a killzone, not in dead hours.

Session Range Boxes

What it is: The indicator can box Tokyo/London/NY ranges and print their highs/lows.

How we use it: Session highs/lows often act like intraday BSL/SSL.

VWAP

What it is: Volume Weighted Average Price — an intraday “fair price” anchor.

How we use it: Quick bias filter: above VWAP = buyers have advantage; below VWAP = sellers have advantage. We still need structure + zones.

ADR / ASR (Average Daily Range / Average Session Range)

ADR: average daily movement; ASR: average range per session (Tokyo/London/NY).

How we use it: If ADR usage is high (≈80–85%+), continuation trades become riskier — we scale targets or avoid chasing.

Daily Pattern Model (Range Day / Buy Day / Sell Day / Continuation)

What it is: A day classification based on PDH/PDL sweeps and reversals.

  • Sell Day: sweep PDH → reverse down (targets SSL/PDL).
  • Buy Day: sweep PDL → reverse up (targets BSL/PDH).
  • Continuation: sweep one side and keep going.
  • Range Day: no clean sweep/shift — choppy.

Confirmation Filters

VPA (Volume Price Analysis)

Core idea: volume is “effort”, price movement is “result”. We want them aligned.

  • Breakout with strong volume → more real.
  • Breakout with low volume → more fake.
  • Pullback into value with decreasing volume → healthier retrace.
  • Pullback with increasing volume against your direction → reversal risk.
RSI (How we use it)

Rule: RSI is a confirmation tool, not an entry trigger.

  • HTF divergence can support reversal stories.
  • On lower TFs, RSI mainly helps confirm momentum (not “overbought/oversold” auto‑reversals).
ADX / Trend Strength

What it is: ADX measures trend strength.

  • ADX > 25: strong trend — continuation setups work better.
  • ADX < 15–20: chop — be picky, avoid forcing scalps.
Trend Cloud & Bar Coloring

Trend Cloud: your long Hull MA cloud (blue/red) for higher‑level bias.

Bar coloring: Supertrend + ADX logic:

  • Blue: bullish bias (stronger when ADX high).
  • Red: bearish bias (stronger when ADX high).
  • Purple: sideways/chop (ADX low).

We still trade structure + zones first — colors are a filter, not the trigger.

SMT Divergence

What it is: One market makes a new high/low, a correlated market fails to confirm.

How we use it: Extra confluence for reversals around liquidity pools (especially near session highs/lows).

SBS (Swing Breakout System)

What it is: A pattern module that detects swing structures and can flag breakouts.

How we use it: As a secondary confirmation — we still demand the main story: liquidity → structure → value → trigger.

Risk & Execution

Entry Trigger (What “confirm” means)
  • Rejection wick from the zone + strong close back in direction.
  • Mini BOS/CHoCH on M5/M15 inside the zone.
  • Volume spike on the rejection candle (optional but powerful).

Never: chase entries after the impulse already left the zone.

Stop Loss (Invalidation)

Rule: SL goes beyond structure, not “random pips”.

  • For shorts: above the swept high / above OB top (depending on setup).
  • For longs: below the swept low / below OB bottom.

We always try to keep SL small, but we won’t force a trade if structure needs a wider invalidation.

Targets (TP1 / TP2 / TP3)
  • TP1: nearest structure / reaction point (take partials, reduce risk).
  • TP2: first major liquidity pool (PDH/PDL/BSL/SSL/EQH/EQL).
  • TP3: next pool or HTF zone (only if the day has room left in ADR).
Risk Planner (XAUUSD pip math)

In the Vanga Sniper planner for XAUUSD: 1 pip = $0.10 price move. 1 lot = 100 oz. That means:

  • 1 pip value for 1.00 lot ≈ $10.
  • If you trade 0.10 lot, 1 pip ≈ $1.

Use it to size positions so your $ risk is controlled — not to “force” entries.
